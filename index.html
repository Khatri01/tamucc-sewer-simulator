About the model

Campus wastewater planning

How will a change in the campus population load the sewer?

Set how many people live, study and work on campus. The simulator follows each person's water use for a week, routes the wastewater through the campus sewer, and shows how full the pipes and pump stations get. Calibrated on Fall 2025 data.

Three steps to a result

  1. Set the scenario. Change the number of resident students, commuter students or faculty and staff, or keep today's campus.
  2. Run it. About two minutes: the agents' water use is simulated, coupled to the sewer model, and routed by EPA SWMM.
  3. Read the answer. Overview gives the flows at the three monitoring stations; Pipe capacity shows how close pipes and pump stations come to their limits.

Run a scenario to see how full the pipes, manholes and pump stations get.

Fullest pipesPeak fullness: highest water depth ÷ pipe diameter. Peak flow ÷ capacity: highest flow ÷ the flow the pipe carries when just full by gravity (set by its size, slope and roughness).
Pump stationswet well's highest level and each pump's share of time running
Manholeswater above the top of the pipes, and flooding

Flows include groundwater infiltration (0.870 cfs in total), which takes up part of every pipe's capacity, most in the lower part of the network. When peak flow ÷ capacity is above 100 %, the pipe runs under pressure: water rises in the manhole above it and pushes the extra flow through. Short spells of this are common in sewers, but a pipe that does it often has no spare capacity for growth. A pipe can also be full with little flow when water backs up from downstream; the table marks those. "Show on map" opens the Sewer network at the moment of the peak, coloured by how full each pipe is.
