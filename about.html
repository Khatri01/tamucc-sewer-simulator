What it simulates

The simulator estimates the dry-weather wastewater flow in the sanitary sewer of Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi. Instead of a fixed daily curve, it follows the people on campus: resident students, commuter students, and faculty and staff. Each person moves between buildings during the day and uses restrooms, showers, laundry and kitchens. Dining-hall kitchens and laboratories add their own water. Every use becomes wastewater in the building where it happens.

That wastewater, plus groundwater that seeps into the pipes, is routed through a calibrated model of the campus sewer network. The results are the flows at three monitoring stations: the residence halls (Station 1), the academic core (Station 2) and the campus outlet (Station 3).

How a run works

  1. Agents. The agent-based model, written in NetLogo, runs two weeks in 15-minute steps. The first week is a warm-up; the results show the second.
  2. Coupling. Each building's wastewater is passed to the manhole it drains to, and groundwater infiltration (0.870 cfs in total) is spread over the pipes that lie below the water table.
  3. Sewer routing. EPA SWMM 5.2.4 routes the flows through the pipes, wet wells and pumps.
  4. Checks. Thirty correctness checks run on every result, including the volume balance: the water the agents generate, plus groundwater, must equal the water that enters the sewer model.

All four steps run in your browser. They use the same model, code and settings as the research version on a desktop computer. In our tests a browser run produced the same sewer-model input and the same station flows, file for file, as the desktop version.

What you can change

Only the number of each kind of agent. Every other parameter keeps its calibrated value; the full list is under “Show model parameters” on the simulator page. Resident students are limited to the beds in the residence halls (3,978). The calibrated numbers (1,824 residents, 9,054 commuter students, 955 faculty and staff) describe the Fall 2025 semester.

Data in this edition

This edition shares, of the campus sewer network, only pipe lengths and diameters and manhole depths. Elevations of manholes and pipes are not shown, and the model files are not offered for download; the results of a run (flows and depths at the three stations, the wastewater generated by source, and the series shown on the map and the building charts) can be downloaded.

Limits

  • Dry weather only. Rain entering the sewer is not modelled.
  • The behaviour of the agents was calibrated to dry-weather flows measured at the three stations in Fall 2025. Very different populations are an extrapolation.
  • The total groundwater infiltration is the average of the lowest flows measured at the campus outlet during low-use periods; how it divides between pipes is an assumption, not a measurement.
  • Each run uses one fixed random seed, so the same numbers always give the same result. That result is one possible outcome: the agents' choices are random, and other seeds give somewhat different flows.
  • These are simulation results for research and teaching. They are not a substitute for an engineering design study.

Privacy

Nothing you enter leaves your browser: there is no server, no account and no tracking. The page downloads its own files and, on the first run, the Python tools from the jsDelivr content network.

Authors and paper

Bishal Khatri and Hua Zhang, Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi. The simulator is based on the framework described in the paper Beyond diurnal curves: An individual-based modelling framework for dry-weather flow.
